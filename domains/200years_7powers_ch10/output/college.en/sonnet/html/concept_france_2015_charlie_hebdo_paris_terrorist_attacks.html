France 2015 Charlie Hebdo Paris Terrorist Attacks

Definition. On January 7, 2015, two gunmen attacked the Paris offices of Charlie Hebdo, a satirical magazine that had published cartoons depicting the Prophet Muhammad, killing twelve people, including much of the publication's senior staff. Related attacks over the following two days killed several more, including a kosher supermarket siege. Ten months later, on November 13, 2015, coordinated attackers struck multiple sites across Paris — the Bataclan concert hall, restaurants, and the Stade de France — killing 130 people. Both attacks were claimed by jihadist organizations (al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula and ISIS, respectively). France declared a state of emergency after the November attacks that was extended repeatedly, granting police expanded search and detention powers and eventually reshaping permanent counterterrorism law.

Application. When you build or read a concept map like this one, ask two questions about any edge marked "composed of" or "related to": (1) Is the link causal (A produces conditions for B), or is it classificatory (A and B belong to the same broader pattern, such as "crises testing European unity")? (2) What is the shortest chain of intermediate events that would make the link causal rather than merely thematic? Here, the answer is that no such chain exists — the honest classification is thematic. Practicing this distinction prevents overreading historical concept maps as strict cause-and-effect diagrams when they are often organizing a period's tensions instead.
