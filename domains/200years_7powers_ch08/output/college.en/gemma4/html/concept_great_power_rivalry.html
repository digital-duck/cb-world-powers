Great Power Rivalry

Great power rivalry refers to the intense competition among major states—those possessing significant military, economic, and diplomatic capacity—for global influence, resources, and geopolitical dominance. Unlike bilateral conflicts, these rivalries are systemic; they fundamentally reshape the international order by forcing all smaller powers to recalculate their strategic positioning. The dominant theoretical framework for understanding this dynamic is Realism, which posits that states operate in an anarchic system where survival is paramount, and power is the ultimate currency. States do not act out of moral obligation or idealism; rather, they are driven by the perceived security dilemma: when one great power accumulates power to enhance its own safety, other powers interpret this accumulation as a threat, necessitating their own counter-accumulation.

Consider the balance of power model. A system is considered stable when no single state can achieve overwhelming dominance ($P_{max} \ll 1$), and instability arises when relative gains are perceived or actualized. The goal of every great power is thus to prevent the formation of a hegemon, maintaining what theorists call the equilibrium.
