Science Cross Cutting Synthesis 1945 2025

Between 1945 and 2025, three overlapping waves of science and technology reshaped the fortunes of the world's leading powers at identifiable turning points, rather than as a steady background hum. The first wave, computing and the internet, began with the transistor (Bell Labs, 1947) and matured through ARPANET (1969), the personal computer boom of the 1980s, and the commercial internet after 1995 — a sequence that moved economic leadership toward whichever country hosted the platforms: the United States through Silicon Valley, and later East Asia through hardware manufacturing. The second wave, genomics, turned biology into an engineerable industry: the Human Genome Project (completed 2003) cut the cost of sequencing a genome from roughly $3 billion to under $600 within two decades, seeding a biotechnology sector that produced the mRNA vaccines deployed within a year of COVID-19's emergence in 2020. The third and still-unresolved wave is artificial intelligence, running from the Perceptron (1958) through AlexNet's 2012 breakthrough in image recognition to large language models like GPT-4 (2023) — a trajectory that concentrates economic and military advantage in whichever nation controls the compute and talent behind it.

A concrete worked example of a leverage point: in October 2022, the United States imposed export controls barring China from importing advanced semiconductor manufacturing equipment. Because AI model training depends on chips built with that equipment, a single policy lever in one technology wave (semiconductors) was used to try to slow a rival's progress in a second wave (AI) — illustrating how the three waves are not independent but interlock.
