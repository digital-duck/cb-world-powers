Given Germany's deep integration into European supply chains but also its historical dependence on energy inputs (formerly Russia, now diversified), Merz’s government must navigate the tension between diplomatic solidarity and economic realism. A key policy mechanism required would be negotiating specific bilateral agreements with major trading blocs that guarantee stable access to critical raw materials (e.g., lithium, rare earth elements), rather than relying solely on pan-European regulatory frameworks. The challenge is thus not merely political consensus, but securing concrete, legally binding trade pathways and establishing a unified national industrial strategy capable of absorbing economic shocks from external geopolitical friction while still fulfilling long-term climate goals.
