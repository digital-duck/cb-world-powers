Concept Book: Science Ai As New Great Power Axis

Payoff

By 2023, artificial intelligence had become something the twentieth century had reserved for only one prior technology: a discovery that was simultaneously reshaping laboratories and reshaping the balance of power between states. Nuclear weapons had done this after 1945 — a physics breakthrough that instantly became the organizing axis of great-power rivalry. Generative AI, arriving through large language models like GPT-4 (2023) and rapid progress in protein-structure prediction tools descended from AlphaFold, did the same. It is not simply that AI is a "big deal" scientifically; it is that AI's emergence coincides, in the historical record, with two other major storylines — American political polarization and Chinese ascendance — turning a scientific chain into a geopolitical one.

This concept is the natural capstone because it braids together the book's three separate threads. Science and mRNA had already shown that breakthrough science (mRNA vaccines, deployed globally within a year of COVID-19's identification in 2019–2020) could move at wartime speed and reshape international dependency, as countries without vaccine-manufacturing capacity relied on the United States, Germany, and later China and India. China's rise as a power supplies the second thread: by 2023, China had become the top filer of AI patents worldwide and a near-peer in AI research output, driving Washington to impose export controls on advanced semiconductors (the 2022 CHIPS Act and subsequent restrictions on Nvidia chip sales to China). American polarization supplies the third: a fractured domestic politics debating AI regulation, job displacement, and disinformation even as the technology becomes a national-security priority — a rare case where a bitterly divided Congress found bipartisan urgency in restricting chip exports.

The result is a genuinely new category: technology as the explicit terrain of great-power competition, not merely its byproduct. Historically, coal, steel, and nuclear fission became strategic once weaponized or industrialized after the fact; AI was declared strategic on arrival, with export controls and national AI strategies announced before the technology had even matured.

This concept, in turn, unlocks the science cross-cutting synthesis 1945–2025 — the exercise of tracing how science has repeatedly reorganized global power for eight decades, from the atom to the algorithm. As practice, compare the 2022–23 chip-export controls to the 1940s nuclear secrecy regime: what did each restrict, and why did policymakers judge information itself worth controlling?
