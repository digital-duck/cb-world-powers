Definition. On February 16, 2024, Alexei Navalny — Russia's most prominent opposition figure and anti-corruption campaigner — died in "Polar Wolf," an Arctic penal colony above the Arctic Circle, while serving a sentence widely viewed as politically motivated. Russian authorities cited natural causes; independent observers, foreign governments, and Navalny's own team pointed to the extreme conditions of his imprisonment (isolation, cold, denial of medical care) as a form of state-facilitated killing. One month later, in March 2024, Vladimir Putin secured a fifth presidential term with roughly 87% of the vote, in an election where credible opposition candidates were barred from the ballot, independent media access was restricted, and monitoring by international observers was minimal.

Worked example. To understand why this sequence matters analytically rather than just as a news event, treat it as a case study in authoritarian consolidation: the systematic removal of channels through which a regime could be challenged. Break the timeline into three linked mechanisms — (1) elimination of the opposition's most visible leader, removing a rallying point capable of mobilizing dissent even from prison; (2) procedural exclusion of remaining challengers from the ballot, ensuring no viable alternative reaches voters; (3) managed information environment, limiting the public's ability to learn about or organize around alternatives. Each mechanism reinforces the others — together they explain how an election can be held, with high turnout and a decisive margin, while providing no meaningful choice.

Problem-solving application. This framework is directly transferable: when analyzing any election result from an authoritarian or hybrid regime, don't stop at the reported vote share — audit the three mechanisms above. Ask: Was genuine opposition leadership present or systematically removed? Were competing candidates permitted meaningful ballot access? Was information about alternatives freely available to voters?
