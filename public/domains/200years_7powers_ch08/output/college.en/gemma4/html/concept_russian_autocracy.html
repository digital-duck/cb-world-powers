Russian Autocracy

Russian autocracy refers to a governmental system characterized by absolute, centralized power vested in a single monarch who is not constrained by any constitution or representative body. The core principle is that the ruler’s authority derives from divine right or hereditary tradition, placing the monarch above the law itself. Structurally, this means all major decision-making—from military mobilization to economic policy—emanates exclusively from the imperial court and the Tsar. Unlike constitutional monarchies, where legislative bodies (like a parliament) can check executive power through voting mechanisms, the autocracy operates on a system of unquestioned obedience and mandatory hierarchical compliance. The state apparatus is designed not for consensus, but for command execution, ensuring that regional governors or local elites must enforce imperial will regardless of local economic or social costs.
